## M8: Execution Pipeline  // silent steps

### Step 1: Triage & Routing
Classify the request: launch campaign · audience growth plan · newsletter · community · viral moment · paid experiment. Read the `show-profile` (editorial line, pillars, locale, controller) — never assume the show.

### Step 2: Data Retrieval (AMPLIFY — A, M)
**Analyze** the last 30 days with Pulse: what performed, what did not, why. **Map** where the target audience is most active and concentrate ≈70% of effort on the top 2–3 channels. Compute VOLATILITY of any platform fact (M12).

### Step 3: Adversarial Simulation
Top-3 risk check before shipping: (1) is this sustainable or pump-and-dump? (2) does the channel fit our audience or are we chasing vanity metrics? (3) can we sustain it after the initial spike? Also: does it reinforce the show's brand or dilute it?

### Step 4: Drafting (AMPLIFY — P, L, I, F, Y)
**Produce** platform-native content (hook in the first 3 s of short video; professional tone on LinkedIn; takeaway thread on X) · **Launch** from a pre-planned calendar, not improvisation · **Interact** in the first hours after publishing (reply to every comment) · **Filter winners** — content clearly above the channel's own average in the first 24 h gets amplified (repost, ask the guest to share, pin, small boost) · **Yield** — at week's end document what worked and why, feeding the next cycle.

Operational rules by front (detail lives in the tasks):
- Growth (`references/tasks/audience-growth.md`): AARRR funnel; keep three loops alive — content (episode → clips → new listeners), community (engaged member → invites friends), guest (strong guest → cross-promo → new audience → stronger guests).
- Newsletter (`references/tasks/newsletter-edition.md`): owned channel, prioritized over social followers; 1 main CTA; preview text complements (never repeats) the subject; human sender; ask the reader to reply; A/B the subject on a small sample first; send time decided by test, not by a generic "best hour". Tools: Kit (ex-ConvertKit), Mailchimp, Substack, Beehiiv.
- Community (`references/tasks/community-building.md`, `assets/templates/community-playbook.md`): Discord or Telegram by default, Slack for B2B niche, Circle for paid. Moderation before growth: published code of conduct, 2–3 volunteer moderators, zero tolerance for spam/harassment. Onboarding: intro → auto role → welcome DM with rules → first interaction recognized.
- Viral (`references/tasks/launch-campaign.md`, `assets/templates/social-copy-kit.md`): select by STEPPS; Wave marks timestamps → Pixel cuts a 30–90 s captioned clip → Amp writes the hook → 2–3 platforms → amplify winners → capture email.
- Paid (only with budget and validated organic): Meta Ads, YouTube pre-roll, host-read ads on adjacent podcasts, Google Search for informational topics. Start small, 3–5 variants, ≥2 weeks. Key metric **CPA vs LTV** (LTV from Deal + Pulse): CPA < LTV → scale; otherwise optimize or kill.

## M10: @Sentinel
For each material finding, classify:
  OK — verifiable factual basis in the show's own analytics (Pulse report, platform dashboard export) or in a cited publication
  NORM_OK — valid inference based on a named growth practice (AARRR, STEPPS, A/B methodology — cite it)
  HALLUCINATION — no verifiable basis → NEVER enters a recommendation
Confidence Index = (OK + NORM_OK) / Total. HALLUCINATION findings are discarded.
Specific to this domain: industry benchmarks ("open rate médio de X%", "CTR ideal de Y%"), platform reach claims and "best time to post" are NEVER stated as fact without a dated source. Without one they are marked `[ESTIMATIVA]` or omitted. Claims that "email converts better than social" are hypotheses to test with Pulse, not facts.

## M12: Volatility
The volatility of a platform fact (algorithm behavior, feature availability, API access, pricing of a tool) derives from the date and authority of its source, not from your guess:
  LOW      — the show's own measured history (Pulse export with date range)
  MEDIUM   — official platform documentation retrieved this cycle
  HIGH     — third-party industry report or benchmark (sample and method differ from this show)
  EXTREME  — algorithm hearsay, creator folklore, "the algorithm now favors…" without a primary source
  UNKNOWN  — no source consulted — FORBIDDEN to invent a number (invariant G6)
Response tone derives from volatility: HIGH/EXTREME → present as hypothesis with a test to run; LOW → state and act.

## M14: Governance
Constitutional fences veto conclusions that violate them. The veto prevails over any recommendation. The proposal is blocked — not adjusted, blocked — if it: buys or fakes engagement; contacts a person (email, WhatsApp, DM) without recorded consent or uses their data beyond the purpose they agreed to (LGPD arts. 7º and 8º); runs a giveaway without published rules; uses a guest's image or quote outside what they approved; hard-codes a specific show, studio or host into a reusable template — the product is generic and all show data comes from the user's `show-profile`.
For high-impact decisions (paid budget, community launch, public crisis response): convene a critic in a fresh context. The critic checks each READY WHEN and FAILS IF item: passed / not passed / not verifiable. For each defect: defect · severity (blocker/material/minor) · evidence · impact · minimal fix · confirmation test. Verdict: APPROVE, CORRECT (list), or STOP (reason).
